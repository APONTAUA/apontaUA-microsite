## 1. Meeting purpose

Clarify the legal and technical context for ApontaUA features, particularly artificial intelligence, personal data protection, institutional authentication and information security.

## 2. AI Act and artificial intelligence

The AI Act analysis should focus on AI features integrated into the product, rather than tools used by the team during development. Examples included chatbots, automatic content generation and research agents. Publishing externally produced AI material is distinct from ApontaUA providing AI features. Authorship, copyright and publication responsibility remain relevant to submitted materials.

**Follow-up:** Confirm whether the product scope includes content generation or other integrated AI features and update the AI Act analysis accordingly.

## 3. GDPR — DPO and GDPR point of contact

The meeting distinguished the formal Data Protection Officer (DPO), whose role is defined by the GDPR, from an internal GDPR point of contact. Daniel was discussed as a possible point of contact; this does not formally appoint him as DPO. The need for a DPO depends on the applicable legal conditions.

**Follow-up:** Confirm the internal point of contact and the entity responsible for formal data-protection duties.

## 4. Data Protection Impact Assessment

A DPIA identifies processing activities, assesses necessity and proportionality, evaluates risks to individuals and defines mitigations. Catarina Silva considered that a formal DPIA might not be required for the scope presented. The MS3 template exercise is not, by itself, proof of a legal obligation.

**Follow-up:** Document the DPIA assessment based on the data and processing actually planned.

## 5. University authentication — UA IdP

Institutional authentication has been used in comparable projects. ApontaUA should state which data it receives and retains from the University of Aveiro identity provider; name and institutional email are personal data. Allowing external accounts would require reassessing collected data, purposes and risks.

**Follow-up:** Document the attributes received from the IdP and confirm whether access is limited to institutional accounts.

## 6. NIS2 and ISO/IEC 27001

NIS2 should not be presented as directly applicable to this academic project in the scope discussed. ISO/IEC 27001 was identified as a good-practice reference for information security, including risk management, access control, information protection and continuity. The team must not claim certification or full compliance. NIS2 and ISO/IEC 27001 are distinct instruments.

**Follow-up:** Keep ISO/IEC 27001 as a security reference and avoid claims of certification.

## 7. Actions and open follow-ups

- Confirm the scope of AI features and update the AI Act analysis.
- Confirm Daniel's internal GDPR contact role without describing it as a formal DPO appointment.
- Document the grounds for conducting or not conducting a formal DPIA.
- Identify personal data received and retained through the UA IdP.
- Confirm whether access is institutional only or includes external accounts.
- Update the presentation and legal documents to distinguish NIS2 from ISO/IEC 27001.

## 8. Summary

The meeting clarified several legal and security topics relevant to MS3. The product's AI scope, formal data-protection responsibilities, DPIA assessment and IdP attributes remain to be confirmed. These notes record the discussion and should not be treated as final institutional decisions without validation.
